\title{Beyond Seen Mistakes: A Matched Test of Natural Error-Composition\\Generalization in Exercise Assessment}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}\\\IEEEauthorblockA{Under double-blind review}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Automated exercise assessment predicts several biomechanical criteria at once,
yet grouped evaluation does not isolate whether a familiar diagnosis was present
in training. We introduce a recording-pair-disjoint matched test on public
ALEX-GYM-1 poses. For each of 12 natural diagnoses, target-present and
target-absent models use identical test recordings, validation data,
initialization, class weights, and training-set size. Across four temporal
backbones---TCN, BiGRU, Transformer, and state-space---an optimized exchange
minimizes residual criterion-marginal differences. Removing target-diagnosis
examples then lowers exact diagnosis match from 22.65\% to 1.52\% (target
bootstrap gap 21.13 points, 95\% CI $[9.86,34.73]$; two-sided $p=.00049$); bit
accuracy falls 12.80 points. Every target falls, and the largest gaps occur
where residual imbalance is smallest. We then replicate the whole protocol on
CPR-Coach, which annotates 13 error types and releases naturally co-occurring
composites: across 72 eligible compositions the gap is 11.80 points, 95\% CI
$[9.03,14.77]$, inside the ALEX-GYM-1 interval but six times more precisely
bounded, and the primary test is no longer pinned to its own floor. A label-space
audit finds that one-bit opposing neighbors predict vulnerable criteria across
models, yet add nothing once ordinary criterion-state support is controlled for
($p=.200$ on ALEX-GYM-1; $p=.87$ over 936 units on CPR-Coach). Finally, the
second dataset exposes a representation defect the first conceals: resampling
every repetition to a fixed frame count erases the quantity that rate-defined
criteria measure, leaving four CPR criteria 14.50 points \emph{below} their own
trivial baselines. Restoring duration, speed and cadence recovers them and
raises exact match from 15.72\% to 37.47\%. The contribution is a controlled
composition test, replicated externally, and an evidence-bounded diagnosis of
its failure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Fine-grained exercise assessment differs from ordinary action recognition. A
system must report several simultaneous errors---for example foot orientation,
hip position, back posture, and movement depth---rather than one action class.
Consequently, a label is a binary diagnosis vector
$\mathbf y\in\{0,1\}^{C}$, and the practically relevant question is whether
individually familiar criterion states remain recognizable when they occur in a
new combination.

The distinction is easy to miss. A recording-disjoint random split protects
against source overlap but does not deliberately hold out a chosen diagnosis
vector, so a model can obtain reasonable marginal accuracy while using pose
patterns correlated with entire diagnoses. This matters for feedback: one
systematically wrong criterion invalidates an otherwise plausible diagnosis.

Composite errors have been studied directly by CPR-Coach, which trains on
isolated error classes and tests synthetically enumerated composites
\cite{wang2024cpr}. Held-out label combinations are also established in
multi-label learning \cite{chai2024compositional}. Our setting is narrower and
naturalistic: training remains multi-error, every evaluated criterion state has
training support, and test diagnoses are naturally observed in a public
two-view exercise dataset, so we do not claim the first composite-error task
or combination split. The difference is the control: a conventional composition
split asks whether unseen combinations are harder, whereas we hold the
evaluation recordings fixed and ask whether they become harder when that one
diagnosis is removed from otherwise supported training.

We organize the study around two retrospective questions. \textbf{RQ1} asks
whether removing a target diagnosis from training reduces performance on the
same held-out recording groups under matched training size and loss weights.
\textbf{RQ2} asks whether local one-bit label structure identifies vulnerable
criteria beyond ordinary marginal criterion support. Tests are two-sided; exact
match was fixed as the sole primary endpoint before the corrected matched rerun.

We contribute (i) a data-audited observed-composition protocol, (ii) a
controlled target-present/absent comparison, (iii) an external replication of
both research questions on a second, independently collected composite-error
dataset, (iv) evidence that a plausible local label diagnostic is subsumed by
ordinary per-criterion class imbalance, and (v) the finding that fixed-length
temporal resampling, a near-universal preprocessing step, silently removes the
evidence for rate-defined criteria. Figure
\ref{fig:protocol} shows the naturally observed diagnosis spaces and targets
retained by the support rule.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Exercise assessment and composite errors}
ALEX-GYM-1 provides two-view 3D poses and multi-criterion exercise annotations
\cite{hassan2025alexgym}. Other rehabilitation datasets emphasize clinical scores,
correctness, or multi-task assessment rather than natural held-out multi-error
label sets \cite{capecci2019kimore,vakanski2018uiprmd,li2024finerehab}.
FineDiving and FineParser study procedure-aware action quality at fine temporal
granularity \cite{xu2022finediving,xu2024fineparser}, but not this diagnosis-set
shift. CPR-Coach is the closest direct composition formulation
\cite{wang2024cpr}; its isolated-error training regime differs from our naturally
multi-error training pool.

Motion-focused tokenization reduces background-driven video domain shift
\cite{liu2026lmft}, an adjacent evidence-selection principle rather than a
baseline for pose input with no background.

\subsection{Multi-label shortcuts and unseen combinations}
\label{sec:shortcuts}
Multi-label models often exploit label dependence. Compositional multi-label
classification explicitly holds out label combinations \cite{chai2024compositional},
while counterfactual and correlation-shortcut work studies harmful co-occurrence
dependence \cite{xie2024counterfactual,fan2023cftc}. We use this literature to
motivate a diagnostic, not a causal claim: label counts characterize exposure in
the benchmark and never enter the exercise classifier. Opposing-state support is
target-relative criterion-state imbalance, an ordinary quantity whose link to
degraded minority-state generalization is established
\cite{buda2018systematic,he2009imbalance,cui2019classbalanced}; our contribution
is showing it subsumes a more localized label-neighborhood account under
composition shift.

\section{Data and Protocol}
\subsection{Dataset and preprocessing audit}
The public ALEX-GYM-1 subset contains squat, Romanian deadlift, and lunge
repetitions with frontal and lateral 33-joint 3D pose sequences. We convert each
trainer criterion to an error bit: one denotes a violated criterion. Table
\ref{tab:data} summarizes the processed data.

All-zero frames occur in both views, ranging from 1.95\% (lunge frontal) to
14.87\% (squat lateral); per-exercise, per-view counts are released. We linearly
interpolate whole missing frames before pelvis centering, robust body-scale
normalization, and resampling to 16 frames. One squat repetition has no valid
frontal frame and is excluded. Raw-file SHA-256 hashes, the exclusion, and
per-sequence quality counts are released. Isolated zero joints are not imputed,
since their semantics cannot be verified.

\subsection{Observed-composition holdout}
For exercise $e$, let $s_i$ identify a paired frontal/lateral recording and
$\mathbf y_i$ its diagnosis. For target diagnosis $\mathbf y^*$, the test set is
all repetitions with $\mathbf y_i=\mathbf y^*$. Every recording group containing
that target is removed from the train/validation pool, including its non-target
repetitions. The remaining groups are split deterministically. Candidate splits
are rejected unless both states of every criterion occur at least eight times in
training and at least once in validation. Train--validation, train--test, and
validation--test group intersections are zero in every fold.

A target is eligible when it has at least ten repetitions, three source groups,
and a feasible supported split. Seven squat and five deadlift diagnoses qualify.
Two lunge candidates are infeasible under that rule---their minority pool
holds exactly eight examples---and are excluded rather than evaluated under a
weaker one. The primary experiment is therefore 12 targets $\times$ three
seeds per model.

Ordinary validation is a secondary comparator, not a pure seen-composition
control: 409 of 1,760 validation rows (23.2\%) have a diagnosis absent from
that fold's training subset.

\subsection{Matched composition-presence test}
RQ1 instead holds the evaluation data fixed. For each target and seed, one target
recording group is test-only; the three seeds select distinct groups. Validation
uses groups containing no target repetition. The \emph{present} training set
contains target repetitions from the remaining target groups. The \emph{absent}
set exchanges those rows for an equal number of non-target rows from the same
training-group pool. Because every eligible replacement differs from the target
in at least one bit, exact criterion-marginal equality is impossible. Before
training, a deterministic binary optimization therefore minimizes the largest
per-criterion positive-count difference and then their sum. Relative to random
exchange, their fold-mean maximum falls from 22.06 to 10.89 counts and their
total from 55.08 to 33.42. Thus the conditions have identical size, validation
and test rows, initialization, shared class weights, and zero group overlap,
with reduced but nonzero marginal differences. This test uses four generic temporal
backbones and averages seeds, then models, within each diagnosis.

\subsection{External replication on CPR-Coach}
\label{sec:cpr}
ALEX-GYM-1 alone cannot show that the effect is not a property of one dataset,
so we repeat the entire protocol on CPR-Coach \cite{wang2024cpr}, which
annotates 13 error types and releases recordings in which two, three or four
co-occur. Only its released 2D pose keypoints and labels are used. Each take is
recorded on four synchronised channels, which are concatenated into one
repetition rather than treated as four rows. Poses are Halpe body joints; the 42
hand keypoints are discarded because their mean detector confidence is .058
against .606 for the body, so three hand-placement criteria are effectively
unobservable from the released skeletons. The recording group is the
composition-pure class folder, of which every eligible composition has exactly
three, matching the three-seed distinct-test-group rule.

Applying the same frozen support rule yields 72 eligible compositions---13
single-error and 59 double-error---from 1{,}414 repetitions, against 12 on
ALEX-GYM-1. The larger pool also balances better: the optimized exchange cuts
the fold-mean worst-criterion mismatch from 10.78 to 3.71 counts, roughly three
times smaller than is achievable on ALEX-GYM-1. Labels are sparser here (1.67
positives of 13), so an all-zero predictor already scores 87.17\% bit accuracy
against 2.97\% exact match; exact match therefore remains the primary endpoint
and bit accuracy is read against that baseline throughout.

\subsection{Models and training}
We use six compact controlled models, all receiving the same $16\times198$
sequence (two views $\times$ 33 joints $\times$ xyz). The four matched
backbones are a dilated TCN, a bidirectional
GRU, a two-layer four-head Transformer encoder, and a bidirectional selective
scan, with 112k, 99k, 190k and 13k deadlift parameters; layer dimensions are in
the released code. The SSM is a compact study implementation, not an official
reproduction. ST-GCN applies two spatial graph/temporal
convolution blocks to each MediaPipe view using the physical skeleton edges,
then fuses pooled view features (35k deadlift parameters).

FACT routes each criterion to the view and joint set its annotation wording
names, through a learned residual on a sigmoid mask, then pools per-view
temporal evidence into one 32-D token per criterion, decoded by a local linear
score plus a learned two-state prototype distance. It never consumes other
labels at inference and has 84,858 squat and 81,048 deadlift parameters. It is
a locality-oriented comparator, not a claimed superior architecture; exact
layer dimensions are in the released code.

Every model receives the same representation and split, with class-weighted
binary cross-entropy, AdamW, fixed 0.5 thresholds, and early stopping on
validation macro-F1. Hyperparameters are never selected using
the held-out target. We report target-equal means: seeds are averaged within
target, then all 12 targets receive equal weight. The full-target and matched
experiments use separate deterministic split constructions; each released
manifest records raw-file hashes, preprocessing version, exact indices, target
support, exclusions, and zero group intersections for every target and seed.

\section{Label-Space Diagnostic}
For criterion $c$, let $\mathbf y^*_{-c}$ denote the other criterion bits. Label-
Opposition Pressure is
\begin{equation}
\pressure_c(\mathbf y^*)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}
\mathbb 1[\mathbf y_{i,-c}=\mathbf y^*_{-c}]
\mathbb 1[y_{i,c}\ne y_c^*],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ is the fold training set. Large pressure means many training
examples reproduce the target context while supporting the wrong current bit.
We compare it with global opposing-state support,
$G_c=\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}\mathbb 1[y_{i,c}\ne y_c^*]$, a single training-label
count requiring no context matching. Target frequency, cardinality, and total
one-bit neighborhood density are constants across criteria inside a diagnosis
and cancel under within-diagnosis ranking. Both LOP and $G_c$ are exposure
associations, not causal influence or a guarantee of failure.

\section{Statistical Analysis}
The held-out diagnosis is the statistical unit. For RQ1, we average seeds and the
four matched architectures within target, then test 12 present--absent differences.
Exact match is primary; bit accuracy and F1 are secondary. For RQ2, we average
all five original temporal models over seeds, compute Spearman correlation within
diagnosis, and take the raw diagnosis-equal mean across 67 target--criterion units.
A 50,000-shuffle test permutes a predictor only among criteria of the same
diagnosis. A partial rank test residualizes LOP and accuracy against $G_c$ and
target-bit state before applying the same blocked permutation. We report
100,000-sample target bootstrap intervals and two-sided probabilities. At 12 targets
the exact two-sided Wilcoxon minimum is $2/2^{12}=.00049$; a $p$ at that value
means all targets moved alike and cannot fall further. Exact
match was fixed before both corrected reruns; the exchange optimization was
specified before examining its model outcomes.

\section{Results}
\subsection{RQ1: matched removal exposes diagnosis dependence}
Under optimized exchange and matched test recordings, target-present exact match
is 22.65\%, versus 1.52\% when the target is absent from training: a 21.13-point
gap (95\% CI $[9.86,34.73]$; two-sided Wilcoxon $p=.00049$). The gap is positive
for every architecture (26.76 TCN, 20.69 BiGRU, 20.14 Transformer, and 16.94 SSM
points). Bit accuracy also falls 12.80 points (CI $[7.38,18.79]$; $p=.00049$).
The original random-exchange analysis gives a compatible 25.44-point exact-match
gap (CI $[17.31,33.32]$). RQ1 is therefore supported without comparing different
evaluation distributions and is robust to substantially improved marginal balance.

Figure \ref{fig:matched} resolves the 12 targets individually. All 12 fall, and
residual mismatch does not track the effect: the two largest gaps (68.61,
65.56 points) sit at two of the smallest residual mismatches (7.7, 3.7 counts),
and the gap--mismatch rank association is negative ($\rho=-.26$), opposite the
confound's prediction. This is evidence against residual marginal mismatch
being the main explanation, not a demonstration that no confounding remains; at
12 targets it is suggestive, not decisive.

\subsection{RQ1 replicates on a second dataset}
Table \ref{tab:replication} repeats the matched test on CPR-Coach. Removing the
target composition lowers exact match from 15.72\% to 3.92\%, a gap of 11.80
points (95\% CI $[9.03,14.77]$), with 62 of 72 compositions falling, 7 tied and
3 reversed. The gap is positive for every backbone (12.57 Transformer, 12.18
BiGRU, 11.78 TCN, 10.67 SSM).

Two things matter more than the point estimate. The CPR-Coach estimate lies
inside the ALEX-GYM-1 interval while being roughly six times narrower, so the
two datasets are statistically compatible rather than merely same-signed. And
with 72 units the primary test is no longer saturated: on ALEX-GYM-1 the exact
Wilcoxon $p$ of $.00049$ \emph{is} the smallest value attainable at $n=12$,
whereas here the exact value is $7.4\times10^{-18}$ against a floor of
$5.4\times10^{-20}$, a factor of 137 above it.

Two architectures added on this dataset do not support the effect. ST-GCN
reaches only 2.22\% exact match against 0.35\%, with 64 of 72 compositions tied
at zero, so it fails the task outright rather than failing it compositionally.
FACT, given the frontal and lateral channels identified from measured geometry,
shows no gap under its anatomical prior ($-3.40$ points) and a smaller one under
a random joint map of equal size ($+4.86$); we therefore report no transfer of
its anatomical prior to this dataset.

\subsection{RQ2: marginal support, not local opposition, is the practical predictor}
Raw LOP is negatively associated with held-out criterion accuracy across all five
temporal models (per-model $\rho$ from $-.367$ to $-.648$, all blocked
$p\leq.00584$); the model-mean $\rho$ is $-.632$ ($p<.00004$). However, global
opposing-state support is stronger ($\rho=-.742$, $p<.00004$). After controlling
for that support and the target bit, LOP's partial rank association is $-.182$
($p=.200$). RQ2 is therefore not supported in its incremental form: the local
count is descriptive, but these data favor the simpler marginal-support account.

The association is practically large: among the 67 target--criterion units,
the bottom $G_c$ quartile (least opposed) averages 82.70\% held-out accuracy
versus 40.79\% for the top quartile (most opposed). $G_c$ is an ordinary, target-relative imbalance count over
training labels, so this confirms---rather than introduces---a training-free
audit signal for a proposed composition, available without running an experiment.

The negative result replicates, and more decisively. Over 936 target--criterion
units on CPR-Coach, LOP's partial rank association after the same controls is
$-.005$ ($p=.87$); the pre-specified sensitivity that drops the three
unobservable hand criteria gives $+.020$ ($p=.605$) over 720 units. Raw
associations are consistent across all five architectures there ($-.231$ to
$-.316$, each $p=10^{-4}$). One claim does not carry over and we do not restate
it: on ALEX-GYM-1 marginal support was the stronger raw predictor
($\rho=-.742$ against $-.632$), whereas on CPR-Coach that ordering reverses
($-.255$ against $-.316$). What survives on both datasets is the null---local
opposition adds nothing once ordinary support is controlled---not the ranking of
the two raw predictors.

\subsection{Fixed-length resampling erases rate-defined criteria}
Resampling every repetition to 16 frames normalizes the time axis, which removes
the quantity that criteria such as ``Slow Frequency'' or ``Insufficient
Pressing'' are defined by: a two-second and a six-second repetition become the
same tensor. Measured against each criterion's own trivial baseline on
CPR-Coach, the four rate-defined criteria score 14.50 points \emph{below} it
while the six postural criteria score 7.71 above---the models do worse than
guessing exactly where the evidence has been deleted.

Restoring three statistics computed before resampling---log duration, mean
displacement per original frame, and the dominant non-DC frequency of the
highest-variance coordinate---recovers them. Rate criteria gain 17.73 points of
lift while postural criteria move 0.67 and hand criteria $-0.56$, so the
channels supply rate information rather than label information. Exact match rises
from 15.72\% to 37.47\% and the composition gap from 11.80 to 27.15 points, with
70 of 72 compositions falling; bit accuracy rises from 86.27\%, below the
all-zero baseline, to 91.90\% above it. On ALEX-GYM-1 the same channels change
nothing (gap 21.13 to 19.05 points, rate-worded criteria $-2.12$), which is
consistent: squat and deadlift criteria are largely geometric, whereas chest
compression is intrinsically a rate task. The defect is therefore severe for
rate-defined criteria and immaterial for postural ones---a property of the
annotation vocabulary, not of either dataset.

\section{Discussion}
The matched comparison changes the evidential strength: target presence is
varied while evaluation recordings and training budget are fixed. The effect
appears in convolutional, recurrent, attention, and state-space models, and
ST-GCN's low full-target exact match shows explicit skeleton topology is not by
itself a remedy.

The control analysis also changes the mechanism claim. LOP reliably describes
which bit fails, but it is largely a proxy for target-relative criterion-state
imbalance (Sec.~\ref{sec:shortcuts}). The practical takeaway is therefore not a
label-neighborhood mechanism but that this ordinary quantity, on its own,
predicts held-out failure well enough to serve as a deployment-time check
requiring no training run.

The second dataset does more than widen coverage. It moves the primary test off
its own floor, turns a same-signed result into a statistically compatible one,
and---because its annotation vocabulary is rate-heavy where ALEX-GYM-1's is
geometric---exposes a preprocessing defect that a single dataset of either kind
would have hidden. That the same three channels are decisive on one dataset and
inert on the other is itself the evidence that the defect tracks what a
criterion measures rather than which corpus it came from.

\section{Limitations}
Two datasets is still not many, and both remain recording-disjoint rather than
participant-disjoint: neither release carries verified participant identities,
so repeated performers may span folds. Three CPR-Coach criteria are effectively
unobservable from its released skeletons, and its compositions are thinner
(mostly pairs) than ALEX-GYM-1's. FACT and ST-GCN results on CPR-Coach come
from a reduced protocol---one seed for FACT---and its anatomical joint map is
our own reading of the domain, which is why the random-map control is reported
beside it. The matched test uses three test groups per diagnosis holding 1--5 repetitions
(median 5), making per-fold exact match coarse, and exchanges examples rather
than preserving identical marginals; optimization
roughly halves the mean worst-criterion difference, but residual imbalance
prevents a causal interpretation. Manual criterion--anatomy mappings affect
FACT, which is why it is only a comparator. Thresholds are fixed at 0.5, with
no calibrated-probability claim. Missing-frame rates reach 14.87\% in the
squat lateral view; interpolation reduces but cannot eliminate pose-detector
uncertainty. The $G_c$ quartile split (17 units/quartile) reuses the RQ2
sample, so it is descriptive, not independent validation. Exact match is
clinically intuitive but harsh; we also report bit accuracy and micro-F1.

\section{Conclusion}
On identical held-out recordings, removing a natural diagnosis from matched
training reduces exact match by 21.13 points on ALEX-GYM-1 and 11.80 points
across 72 compositions of CPR-Coach, the two intervals overlapping and the
second no longer pinned to the smallest value its test can return. Label-space
analysis predicts which criteria fail, but local one-bit opposition adds nothing
beyond ordinary criterion-state support on either dataset. The second corpus
also shows that resampling repetitions to a fixed frame count erases the
evidence for rate-defined criteria, pushing them below their own trivial
baselines until duration, speed and cadence are restored. The result is a
reproducible composition test, externally replicated, an evidence-bounded
diagnosis of its failure, and a preprocessing caution for any multi-criterion
assessment whose vocabulary names a rate.
