\title{Beyond Seen Mistakes: A Matched Test of Natural Error-Composition\\Generalization in Exercise Assessment}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}\\\IEEEauthorblockA{Under double-blind review}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Automated exercise assessment predicts several biomechanical criteria at once,
yet grouped evaluation does not isolate whether a familiar diagnosis was present
in training. We introduce a recording-pair-disjoint matched test on public
ALEX-GYM-1 poses. For each of 12 natural diagnoses, target-present and
target-absent models use identical test recordings, validation data,
initialization, class weights, and training-set size. Across TCN, BiGRU,
Transformer, and state-space backbones, an optimized exchange minimizes residual
criterion-marginal differences. Removing target-diagnosis examples then lowers
exact diagnosis match from 22.65\% to 1.52\% (target bootstrap gap 21.13 points,
95\% CI $[9.86,34.73]$; two-sided $p=.00049$); bit accuracy falls 12.80 points.
A six-model stress test, including ST-GCN and an anatomical-token model, shows
that topology and locality do not eliminate the failure. A label-space audit
finds that one-bit opposing neighbors predict vulnerable criteria across models,
but a far simpler statistic---the raw count of training examples opposing a
criterion's target state---is a stronger predictor ($\rho=-.742$) and absorbs
the local count's association entirely ($p=.200$ once controlled), separating
an 82.70\% bottom-quartile criterion from a 40.79\% top-quartile one. The
contribution
is therefore a controlled composition test, an evidence-bounded diagnosis of
its failure, and a zero-cost pre-training audit statistic that flags which
criteria of a planned diagnosis are likely to fail---not a new architecture or
causal shortcut claim.
\end{abstract}

exercise assessment, multi-label classification, compositional generalization,

\section{Introduction}
Fine-grained exercise assessment differs from ordinary action recognition. A
system must report several simultaneous errors---for example foot orientation,
hip position, back posture, and movement depth---rather than one action class.
Consequently, a label is a binary diagnosis vector
$\mathbf y\in\{0,1\}^{C}$, and the practically relevant question is whether
individually familiar criterion states remain recognizable when they occur in a
new combination.

The distinction is easy to miss. A recording-disjoint random split protects
against direct source overlap, but it does not deliberately hold out a chosen
diagnosis vector. A model can therefore obtain reasonable marginal accuracy
while using pose patterns correlated with entire diagnoses. This matters for
feedback: one systematically wrong criterion invalidates an otherwise plausible
diagnosis.

Composite errors have been studied directly by CPR-Coach, which trains on
isolated error classes and tests synthetically enumerated composites
\cite{wang2024cpr}. Held-out label combinations are also established in
multi-label learning \cite{chai2024compositional}. Our setting is narrower and
naturalistic: training remains multi-error, every evaluated criterion state has
training support, and test diagnoses are naturally observed in a public
two-view exercise dataset. We do not claim the first composite-error task or the
first combination split.

We organize the study around two retrospective questions. \textbf{RQ1} asks
whether removing a target diagnosis from training reduces performance on the
same held-out recording groups under matched training size and loss weights.
\textbf{RQ2} asks whether local one-bit label structure identifies vulnerable
criteria beyond ordinary marginal criterion support. Tests are two-sided; exact
match was fixed as the sole primary endpoint before the corrected matched rerun.

We contribute (i) a data-audited observed-composition protocol, (ii) a
controlled target-present/absent comparison, (iii) a six-architecture stress
test, and (iv) evidence that a plausible local label diagnostic is subsumed by
a much simpler statistic---the marginal count of training examples opposing a
criterion's target state---which doubles as a practical, zero-cost audit
computed from training labels alone before any model is touched. Figure
\ref{fig:protocol} shows the naturally observed diagnosis spaces and targets
retained by the support rule.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Exercise assessment and composite errors}
ALEX-GYM-1 provides two-view 3D poses and multi-criterion exercise annotations
\cite{hassan2025alexgym}. Other rehabilitation datasets emphasize clinical scores,
correctness, or multi-task assessment rather than natural held-out multi-error
label sets \cite{capecci2019kimore,vakanski2018uiprmd,li2024finerehab}.
FineDiving and FineParser study procedure-aware action quality at fine temporal
granularity \cite{xu2022finediving,xu2024fineparser}, but not this diagnosis-set
shift. CPR-Coach is the closest direct composition formulation
\cite{wang2024cpr}; its isolated-error training regime differs from our naturally
multi-error training pool.

Learnable Motion-Focused Tokenization (LMFT) drops low-motion RGB patches to
reduce background-driven video domain shift \cite{liu2026lmft}. Our normalized
pose input contains no scene background, while static joint configurations can
define form errors. LMFT is therefore an adjacent evidence-selection principle,
not a directly matched method or baseline for diagnosis-composition shift.

\subsection{Multi-label shortcuts and unseen combinations}
Multi-label models often exploit label dependence. Compositional multi-label
classification explicitly holds out label combinations \cite{chai2024compositional},
while counterfactual and correlation-shortcut work studies harmful co-occurrence
dependence \cite{xie2024counterfactual,fan2023cftc}. We use this literature to
motivate a diagnostic, not a causal claim: label counts characterize exposure in
the benchmark and never enter the exercise classifier.

\section{Data and Protocol}
\subsection{Dataset and preprocessing audit}
The public ALEX-GYM-1 subset contains squat, Romanian deadlift, and lunge
repetitions with frontal and lateral 33-joint 3D pose sequences. We convert each
trainer criterion to an error bit: one denotes a violated criterion. Table
\ref{tab:data} summarizes the processed data.

All-zero frames occur in both views: squat has 183/4,720 frontal (3.88\%) and
702/4,720 lateral frames (14.87\%); deadlift has 250/4,304 (5.81\%) and
326/4,304 (7.57\%); and lunge has 33/1,696 (1.95\%) and 214/1,696 (12.62\%),
respectively. We linearly
interpolate whole missing frames before pelvis centering, robust body-scale
normalization, and resampling to 16 frames. One squat repetition has no valid
frontal frame and is excluded. Raw-file SHA-256 hashes, the exclusion, and
per-sequence quality counts are released. We do not impute isolated zero joints,
whose semantics cannot be verified from the release.

\subsection{Observed-composition holdout}
For exercise $e$, let $s_i$ identify a paired frontal/lateral recording and
$\mathbf y_i$ its diagnosis. For target diagnosis $\mathbf y^*$, the test set is
all repetitions with $\mathbf y_i=\mathbf y^*$. Every recording group containing
that target is removed from the train/validation pool, including its non-target
repetitions. The remaining groups are split deterministically. Candidate splits
are rejected unless both states of every criterion occur at least eight times in
training and at least once in validation. Train--validation, train--test, and
validation--test group intersections are zero in every fold.

A target is eligible when it has at least ten repetitions, three source groups,
and a feasible supported split. Seven squat and five deadlift diagnoses qualify.
Two lunge diagnoses considered by the earlier protocol are infeasible: their
minority pool contains exactly eight examples, so eight cannot remain in training
while one also enters validation. They are excluded rather than evaluated under a
different rule. The primary experiment is therefore 12 targets $\times$ three
seeds per model.

Ordinary validation is retained as a secondary stress-test comparator, but it is
not a pure seen-composition control: 409 of 1,760 validation rows (23.2\%) have a
diagnosis absent from that fold's training subset.

\subsection{Matched composition-presence test}
RQ1 instead holds the evaluation data fixed. For each target and seed, one target
recording group is test-only; the three seeds select distinct groups. Validation
uses groups containing no target repetition. The \emph{present} training set
contains target repetitions from the remaining target groups. The \emph{absent}
set exchanges those rows for an equal number of non-target rows from the same
training-group pool. Because every eligible replacement differs from the target
in at least one bit, exact criterion-marginal equality is impossible. Before
training, a deterministic binary optimization therefore minimizes the largest
per-criterion positive-count difference and then their sum. Relative to random
exchange, their fold-mean maximum falls from 22.06 to 10.89 counts and their
total from 55.08 to 33.42. Thus the conditions have identical size, validation
and test rows, initialization, shared class weights, and zero group overlap,
with reduced but nonzero marginal differences. This test uses four generic temporal
backbones and averages seeds, then models, within each diagnosis.

\subsection{Models and training}
We use six compact controlled models: a temporal CNN, BiGRU, Transformer,
selective state-space model, ST-GCN, and Factorized Anatomical Criterion Tokens
(FACT).
All baselines receive a $16\times198$ sequence (two views $\times$ 33 joints
$\times$ xyz). The TCN uses a 96-D frame embedding, temporal kernels 5 and 3,
and mean/max pooling. BiGRU uses a 72-D bidirectional GRU; Transformer uses two
96-D, four-head encoder layers; and SSM uses one 32-D bidirectional selective
scan. Their deadlift models contain 112k, 99k, 190k, and 13k parameters,
respectively. The SSM is a compact study implementation rather than an official
reproduction of a named large model. ST-GCN applies two spatial graph/temporal
convolution blocks to each MediaPipe view using the physical skeleton edges,
then fuses pooled view features (35k deadlift parameters).

FACT reshapes each sequence to $2\times16\times33\times3$. For criterion $c$,
the annotation wording specifies one view and an initial joint set. A learned
residual adjusts a sigmoid mask initialized near .95 on mapped joints and .05
elsewhere. Separate frontal/lateral encoders apply a 56-D frame projection,
temporal convolutions of width 5 and 3, and mean/max pooling. A criterion-specific
$112\!\rightarrow\!32$ adapter produces one token per criterion. Its logit sums
a local linear score and a scaled distance difference to two learned 32-D state
prototypes. FACT never consumes other labels or logits at inference and contains
84,858 parameters for squat and 81,048 for deadlift. It is a locality-oriented
comparator, not a claimed generally superior architecture.

Every model receives the same normalized two-view representation and split.
Models use class-weighted binary cross-entropy, AdamW, fixed 0.5 thresholds, and
early stopping on validation macro-F1. Hyperparameters are never selected using
the held-out target. We report target-equal means: seeds are averaged within
target, then all 12 targets receive equal weight. All primary trainers call the
same deterministic constrained splitter. The released manifest records raw-file
hashes, preprocessing version, exact indices, target support, exclusions, and
zero group intersections for every target and seed.

\section{Label-Space Diagnostic}
For criterion $c$, let $\mathbf y^*_{-c}$ denote the other criterion bits. Label-
Opposition Pressure is
\begin{equation}
\pressure_c(\mathbf y^*)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}
\mathbb 1[\mathbf y_{i,-c}=\mathbf y^*_{-c}]
\mathbb 1[y_{i,c}\ne y_c^*],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ is the fold training set. Large pressure means many training
examples reproduce the target context while supporting the wrong current bit.
We compare it with global opposing-state support,
$G_c=\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}\mathbb 1[y_{i,c}\ne y_c^*]$, a single training-label
count requiring no context matching. Target frequency, cardinality, and total
one-bit neighborhood density are constants across criteria inside a diagnosis
and cancel under within-diagnosis ranking. Both LOP and $G_c$ are exposure
associations, not causal influence or a guarantee of failure.

\section{Statistical Analysis}
The held-out diagnosis is the statistical unit. For RQ1, we average seeds and the
four matched architectures within target, then test 12 present--absent differences.
Exact match is primary; bit accuracy and F1 are secondary. For RQ2, we average
all five original temporal models over seeds, compute Spearman correlation within
diagnosis, and take the raw diagnosis-equal mean across 67 target--criterion units.
A 50,000-shuffle test permutes a predictor only among criteria of the same
diagnosis. A partial rank test residualizes LOP and accuracy against $G_c$ and
target-bit state before applying the same blocked permutation. We report
100,000-sample target bootstrap intervals and two-sided probabilities. Exact
match was fixed before both corrected reruns; the exchange optimization was
specified before examining its model outcomes.

\section{Results}
\subsection{RQ1: matched removal exposes diagnosis dependence}
Under optimized exchange and matched test recordings, target-present exact match
is 22.65\%, versus 1.52\% when the target is absent from training: a 21.13-point
gap (95\% CI $[9.86,34.73]$; two-sided Wilcoxon $p=.00049$). The gap is positive
for every architecture (26.76 TCN, 20.69 BiGRU, 20.14 Transformer, and 16.94 SSM
points). Bit accuracy also falls 12.80 points (CI $[7.38,18.79]$; $p=.00049$).
The original random-exchange analysis gives a compatible 25.44-point exact-match
gap (CI $[17.31,33.32]$). RQ1 is therefore supported without comparing different
evaluation distributions and is robust to substantially improved marginal balance.

\subsection{RQ2: marginal support, not local opposition, is the practical predictor}
Raw LOP is negatively associated with held-out criterion accuracy across all five
temporal models (per-model $\rho$ from $-.367$ to $-.648$, all blocked
$p\leq.00584$); the model-mean $\rho$ is $-.632$ ($p<.00004$). However, global
opposing-state support is stronger ($\rho=-.742$, $p<.00004$). After controlling
for that support and the target bit, LOP's partial rank association is $-.182$
($p=.200$). RQ2 is therefore not supported in its incremental form: the local
count is descriptive, but these data favor the simpler marginal-support account.

The association is practically large: among the 67 target--criterion units,
the bottom $G_c$ quartile (least opposed) averages 82.70\% held-out accuracy
versus 40.79\% for the top quartile (most opposed). Because $G_c$ needs only
training labels, this doubles as a pre-training audit for a proposed
diagnosis composition, computed without running an experiment.

\section{Discussion}
The matched comparison changes the evidential strength of the study: target
presence is varied while evaluation recordings and training budget are fixed.
The effect appears in convolutional, recurrent, attention, and state-space
models; ST-GCN's low full-target exact match further shows that explicit skeleton
topology is not by itself a remedy.

The control analysis also changes the mechanism claim. LOP reliably describes
which bit fails, but it is partly a proxy for ordinary criterion-state imbalance.
Reporting the stronger $G_c$ control and null partial result prevents a local
label-neighborhood association from being mistaken for unique or causal
evidence. This reframes the diagnostic contribution rather than weakening it:
the practical takeaway is not a mechanism story about label neighborhoods but
that ordinary criterion-state imbalance, on its own, predicts held-out failure
well enough to serve as a deployment-time checklist item requiring no training
run.

\section{Limitations}
The study has one multi-label composition dataset and only 12 eligible diagnosis
units. The split key identifies paired recordings, not verified participants, so
we claim recording-pair rather than participant separation. The matched test uses
three test groups per diagnosis and exchanges examples rather than preserving
identical criterion marginals. Optimization roughly halves the mean worst-criterion
count difference, but residual imbalance prevents a causal interpretation. Manual
criterion--anatomy mappings affect FACT,
which is why FACT is only a comparator. Thresholds are fixed at 0.5, and no claim
is made about calibrated probabilities. Missing-frame rates reach 14.87\%
in the squat lateral view; interpolation reduces but cannot eliminate uncertainty
from pose-detector failures. The $G_c$ quartile split (17 units per quartile) reuses the RQ2 sample, so it
is descriptive, not an independent validation of the audit statistic. Finally,
exact match is clinically intuitive but harsh; it is reported alongside bit
accuracy and micro-F1.

\section{Conclusion}
On identical held-out recordings, removing a natural diagnosis from matched
training reduces exact match by 21.13 points across four architecture families,
even after criterion-marginal differences are substantially reduced.
ST-GCN and anatomical tokens do not eliminate the broader stress-test gap.
Label-space analysis predicts vulnerable criteria, and the simplest available
statistic---marginal opposing-state support, not local one-bit
opposition---does the predicting. The result is a reproducible composition
test, an evidence-bounded diagnosis of its failure, and a practical,
training-free audit statistic a practitioner can compute before touching a
model.
